Where is your data exposed right now?

The live map plots exposure country by country, with running counters for countries, data leaks and forum posts across every source DarkIntel360 watches.

  • Exposure counters

    100 countries, 6.2K data leaks and 13.2K forum posts in the demo view.

    Counters sit on top of the map and move as new reports land.

  • Top affected countries

    Ranked by exposure volume across every source.

    A running list, from Afghanistan to Bangladesh and on, that you can narrow to any date range.

  • Regional zoom

    6.3K phishing reports in the demo region.

    Zoom into a region and the counters follow, so you see what is happening next door as well as at home.

What are underground forums saying about you?

Forum reports collect posts from the boards where breaches are announced, with the date, the countries named, the views and a credibility score on every one.

  • Forum reports

    Post summary, date, countries, views and credibility on one row.

    Every post becomes a record your team can sort, filter and open.

  • Translated source

    The original post, in English, with its ID and link.

    Open a report to read the source in full. The link stays attached as evidence.

  • Credibility 50 / 100

    Mixed supporting signals.

    Source quality and the evidence around a claim are weighed, so a rumour never looks like a confirmed sale.

Is that breach claim real?

Data leak intelligence lists every incident claim with its attacker, source and timeline, then checks it against supporting signals before it reaches you.

  • Source incident claim

    2.60K users, first breached in November 2012.

    The film’s example: an old forum breach, posted again, with the attacker and source attached.

  • Credibility 86 / 100

    Strong supporting signals.

    When the evidence holds up, the score says so, and the claim moves to the top of the queue.

  • Source details

    Reported, source status, last checked.

    Each claim records when it was posted, whether the source is still reachable and when it was last checked.

Which of your logins are already out there?

Credential records list the URL, username, password, country and date of every exposed login, with the sensitive values blurred until you need them.

  • Exposed logins

    Username and password, per site.

    The exposed-login columns show which accounts are circulating and for which service.

  • Login pages

    The sign-in URL behind every record.

    From design tools to webmail to mobile apps, you see exactly where each login works.

  • Date and country

    When and where each login surfaced.

    Sort by date to see what is fresh, and by country to see where it is spreading.

Is one of your machines infected?

Botnet data lists infected computers from stealer logs: the computer, the user, the country, the IP, how many logins it held and when it was seen.

  • Infected computers

    Computer, user, country, IP and logins count.

    One row per machine, so a home laptop never hides among company workstations.

  • Host details

    System overview, saved logins, browser evidence.

    Open a host to see its profile and every login it saved: 29 on the demo machine.

  • Security and access

    The antivirus it ran, and its status.

    Endpoint context shows what protection the machine had, so you can see how the infection got past it.

Who is hosting the page pretending to be you?

Phishing records put suspicious pages side by side with their submission time, IP address, hosting organisation, ISP and ASN.

  • Hosting trail

    IP address, organisation, ISP and ASN.

    The demo traces one lure to Framer B.V. on Amazon’s AS16509: enough to send the takedown to the right desk.

  • Side by side

    Two lures, one screen.

    Compare a suspect page with another copy and see what changed between them.

  • Country and timezone

    Where the page is served from.

    Each record notes the country, city and timezone of the host, so you know whose jurisdiction you are dealing with.

Which sites like yours are being defaced?

Every defacement is logged with the date, the attacker, the team, the server’s operating system and a mirror of the page as it was.

  • Attacker and team

    A handle and a crew on every row.

    Follow one attacker across targets and see which teams are busy this week.

  • Mirror captures

    The page, exactly as it was defaced.

    Mirrors keep the evidence after the site owner has cleaned up.

  • Server OS

    Linux, Windows or unknown.

    The operating system column hints at how a site was reached, and which of yours run the same.
